# Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

Condition: copd

Diagnostic criteria: progressive dyspnea, chronic cough, and sputum production in a patient with a significant exposure history (typically tobacco smoke or occupational irritants); confirmed by post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 0.70 on spirometry, indicating persistent airflow limitation not fully reversible.

First-line treatment: long-acting bronchodilator (LAMA or LABA) as maintenance therapy for symptomatic patients; short-acting bronchodilators for as-needed relief; add inhaled corticosteroid in those with frequent exacerbations or an asthma-overlap picture; smoking cessation and pulmonary rehabilitation are core non-pharmacologic measures.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: marked increase in dyspnea or sputum purulence with hemodynamic instability, SpO2 significantly below the patient's baseline, confusion or drowsiness, cyanosis, use of accessory respiratory muscles, or signs of cor pulmonale (peripheral edema, jugular venous distension) — these suggest a severe exacerbation or respiratory failure requiring emergency assessment.
