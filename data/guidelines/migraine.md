# Migraine

Condition: migraine

Diagnostic criteria: recurrent moderate-to-severe headache attacks, often unilateral and pulsating, lasting hours to a few days, associated with nausea, photophobia, and/or phonophobia; a subset of patients experience a preceding aura with transient visual, sensory, or speech disturbances; diagnosis is clinical, based on characteristic attack pattern and exclusion of secondary causes.

First-line treatment: simple or combination analgesics (e.g. NSAIDs, acetaminophen) for mild attacks; triptans as first-line abortive therapy for moderate-to-severe attacks in appropriate candidates; antiemetics for associated nausea; preventive therapy (e.g. beta-blockers, certain anticonvulsants, or CGRP-targeted agents) is considered for frequent or disabling attacks.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: sudden "thunderclap" onset, headache with fever and neck stiffness, new neurologic deficit, papilledema, headache after head trauma, or a marked change in a patient's usual headache pattern — these suggest a possible secondary cause (e.g. hemorrhage, meningitis, mass lesion) requiring urgent neurologic evaluation rather than routine migraine management.
