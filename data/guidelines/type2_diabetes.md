# Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

Condition: type2_diabetes

Diagnostic criteria: fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL, HbA1c ≥6.5%, or random glucose ≥200 mg/dL with classic hyperglycemia symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss); often identified incidentally in asymptomatic patients through routine screening given risk factors such as obesity, family history, or prior gestational diabetes.

First-line treatment: lifestyle intervention (dietary modification, weight loss, structured physical activity) alongside metformin as the initial pharmacologic agent for most patients, titrated as tolerated; additional agents (e.g. SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, sulfonylureas, or insulin) are added based on glycemic targets, cardiovascular/renal comorbidities, and tolerability.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: signs of diabetic ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state — including severe hyperglycemia, vomiting, abdominal pain, Kussmaul breathing, fruity breath odor, altered consciousness, or marked dehydration — as well as severe hypoglycemia with confusion or loss of consciousness, all require emergency management.
