# Acute Gastroenteritis

Condition: gastroenteritis

Diagnostic criteria: acute onset of diarrhea (with or without vomiting), abdominal cramping, and low-grade fever, typically self-limited and lasting a few days; usually diagnosed clinically based on symptom pattern and exposure history (e.g. contaminated food/water, sick contacts), without routine need for stool studies in mild cases.

First-line treatment: oral rehydration therapy as the cornerstone of management, alongside continued age-appropriate feeding; antiemetics may be used for troublesome vomiting; antibiotics are generally not indicated for presumed viral gastroenteritis and are reserved for specific bacterial etiologies identified through testing.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: signs of significant dehydration (lethargy, sunken eyes, minimal urine output, dizziness on standing), high fever, bloody diarrhea, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting preventing oral intake, or symptoms in very young, elderly, or immunocompromised patients — these warrant prompt clinical evaluation and possible intravenous rehydration.
