# Community-acquired Pneumonia

Condition: pneumonia

Diagnostic criteria: acute cough, fever, pleuritic chest pain, and dyspnea accompanied by a new infiltrate on chest imaging; supportive findings include focal crackles or dullness on auscultation, tachypnea, and leukocytosis; severity is commonly estimated using clinical scoring tools (e.g. CURB-65) that weigh confusion, renal function, respiratory rate, blood pressure, and age.

First-line treatment: empiric oral antibiotics (e.g. amoxicillin or a macrolide) for low-severity outpatient cases based on local resistance patterns and comorbidities; broader-spectrum intravenous therapy and hospitalization for moderate-to-severe presentations; supportive care with antipyretics, fluids, and oxygen as needed.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: respiratory rate ≥ 30/min, SpO2 < 90% on room air, hypotension or septic shock, altered mental status, multilobar involvement, or a high clinical severity score — these indicate a need for hospitalization or intensive-care-level support.
