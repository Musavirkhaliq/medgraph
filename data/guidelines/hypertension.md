# Essential Hypertension

Condition: hypertension

Diagnostic criteria: sustained elevation of blood pressure above accepted thresholds (commonly cited around ≥130/80 or ≥140/90 mmHg depending on the reference framework used), confirmed on repeated measurements across multiple visits or via home/ambulatory monitoring, in the absence of an identifiable secondary cause.

First-line treatment: lifestyle modification (dietary sodium reduction, weight management, regular physical activity, limited alcohol intake) for all patients; pharmacologic therapy typically starts with a thiazide-type diuretic, ACE inhibitor/ARB, or calcium channel blocker, chosen based on comorbidities, age, and tolerability; combination therapy is often needed to reach target values.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: severely elevated blood pressure accompanied by symptoms such as chest pain, severe headache, visual disturbance, confusion, dyspnea, or focal neurologic deficits — suggestive of hypertensive emergency with potential end-organ damage — warrants immediate evaluation rather than routine outpatient follow-up.
