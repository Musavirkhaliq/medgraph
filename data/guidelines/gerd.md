# Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)

Condition: gerd

Diagnostic criteria: recurrent heartburn and acid regurgitation, often worse after meals or when lying flat, with symptoms typically improving with acid-suppressive therapy; diagnosis is usually clinical in typical presentations, with endoscopy reserved for alarm features or symptoms refractory to initial treatment.

First-line treatment: lifestyle modification (weight loss, avoiding late meals, elevating the head of the bed, limiting trigger foods) combined with a trial of proton pump inhibitor therapy for 4-8 weeks; H2-receptor antagonists or antacids may be used for milder or intermittent symptoms.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: dysphagia, odynophagia, unintentional weight loss, persistent vomiting, evidence of gastrointestinal bleeding (hematemesis, melena), or new-onset symptoms in an older patient are alarm features suggesting a need for prompt endoscopic evaluation rather than continued empiric acid-suppression alone.
