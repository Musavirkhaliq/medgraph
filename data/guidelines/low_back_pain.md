# Acute Low Back Pain (Non-specific)

Condition: low_back_pain

Diagnostic criteria: pain localized to the lumbar region, often following mechanical strain or unclear precipitant, without radiating leg pain, neurologic deficit, or systemic symptoms; diagnosis is clinical, based on history and examination, with imaging generally not indicated in the absence of alarm features.

First-line treatment: reassurance and encouragement to remain active within tolerated limits; NSAIDs or acetaminophen for analgesia; short-term use of muscle relaxants may be considered for significant spasm; structured exercise or physical therapy is recommended for symptoms persisting beyond a few weeks.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: saddle anesthesia, new bowel or bladder dysfunction, progressive motor weakness, bilateral leg symptoms, fever with back pain, unexplained weight loss, or history of significant trauma or malignancy — these suggest possible cauda equina syndrome, spinal infection, fracture, or malignant involvement and require urgent imaging and specialist evaluation.
