# Allergic Rhinitis

Condition: allergic_rhinitis

Diagnostic criteria: recurrent or persistent nasal congestion, clear rhinorrhea, sneezing, and nasal/ocular itching triggered by allergen exposure (e.g. pollen, dust mites, pet dander); often seasonal or perennial in pattern, with a personal or family history of atopy supporting the diagnosis, which is usually made clinically.

First-line treatment: second-generation oral antihistamines and/or intranasal corticosteroid sprays as first-line options for most patients; intranasal antihistamines or decongestants may be added for short-term symptom control; allergen avoidance measures are recommended as an adjunct where practical.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: symptoms such as unilateral nasal obstruction, persistent epistaxis, facial pain/swelling, visual changes, or signs suggestive of a secondary sinus infection or structural abnormality are not typical of simple allergic rhinitis and warrant further evaluation rather than continued empiric allergy treatment.
