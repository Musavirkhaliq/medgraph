# Urinary Tract Infection (Uncomplicated, Adult)

Condition: uti

Diagnostic criteria: acute dysuria, urinary frequency and urgency, and suprapubic discomfort in a patient without fever or flank pain, typically supported by urinalysis findings of pyuria and/or bacteriuria; uncomplicated cases occur in otherwise healthy, non-pregnant individuals with a structurally normal urinary tract.

First-line treatment: short-course oral antibiotics (e.g. nitrofurantoin or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, guided by local resistance patterns) for 3-5 days; increased fluid intake and analgesia (e.g. phenazopyridine for symptomatic relief) may be used adjunctively; culture is generally reserved for recurrent, atypical, or treatment-failure cases.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: fever, flank/costovertebral angle pain, nausea/vomiting, or systemic signs of sepsis suggest pyelonephritis or complicated infection rather than a simple lower urinary tract infection; pregnancy, male sex, immunosuppression, or recurrent/relapsing symptoms also warrant closer evaluation rather than routine empiric self-limited management.
