# Cellulitis (Skin/Soft Tissue Infection)

Condition: cellulitis

Diagnostic criteria: acute onset of localized skin erythema, warmth, swelling, and tenderness, typically with poorly demarcated borders and often affecting an extremity; may be accompanied by low-grade fever or regional lymphadenopathy; diagnosis is primarily clinical, based on physical examination findings.

First-line treatment: oral antibiotics targeting common skin flora (e.g. streptococci and staphylococci, such as cephalexin or dicloxacillin) for uncomplicated cases; elevation of the affected limb and treatment of any identifiable entry site (e.g. tinea, wound) as adjunctive measures; intravenous therapy is reserved for more extensive or rapidly progressing infection.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: rapidly spreading erythema, severe pain disproportionate to examination findings, skin necrosis or bullae, crepitus, high fever with systemic toxicity, or hemodynamic instability — these raise concern for necrotizing soft tissue infection or sepsis and require emergency surgical and medical evaluation.
