# Asthma (Acute Exacerbation & Chronic Management)

Condition: asthma

Diagnostic criteria: recurrent wheeze, cough, chest tightness, and dyspnea, often worse at night or with exercise/allergen exposure; variable expiratory airflow limitation (reduced FEV1/FVC, reversible with bronchodilator).

First-line treatment: short-acting beta-agonist (e.g. albuterol/salbutamol) for acute relief; inhaled corticosteroid as the preferred long-term controller for persistent symptoms; add a short oral corticosteroid course for moderate-severe exacerbations.

Red flags requiring urgent escalation: inability to speak in full sentences, accessory muscle use, SpO2 < 92%, silent chest, altered consciousness, or no improvement after repeated bronchodilator doses — these indicate a life-threatening exacerbation requiring emergency care.

Source: illustrative summary adapted from widely published international asthma management guidance (e.g. GINA), paraphrased for demonstration — not verbatim guideline text.
